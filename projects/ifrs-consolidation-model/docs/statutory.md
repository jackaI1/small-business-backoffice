# Comparative Canadian annual statement pack

I use a synthetic, privately held Canadian federal corporation, Maple Services
Group Inc., with three wholly owned service subsidiaries held from formation.
It elects Canadian GAAP Part I, incorporating IFRS Accounting Standards, with an
October year-end. This is a continuing IFRS reporter, not first-time adoption.
The annual example is separate from the original monthly workpaper example so
both behaviours remain available.

## Reporting basis and jurisdiction

[CBCA section 155](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-44/section-155.html)
requires annual comparative statements for shareholders.
[Corporations Canada](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en/business-corporations/corporate-records-and-other-corporate-obligations)
explains the Canadian Handbook accounting basis and shareholder distribution.
The [IFRS Foundation's Canada profile](https://www.ifrs.org/use-around-the-world/use-of-ifrs-standards-by-jurisdiction/view-jurisdiction/canada/)
confirms private enterprises can elect IFRS within Canadian GAAP.

For years ending in October 2026 and 2025 I use
[IAS 1's statement set](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-1-presentation-of-financial-statements/),
including financial position, profit/OCI, equity changes, cash flows and notes,
with comparative amounts. IFRS 18 is not early adopted. The
[IAS 7 cash flow framework](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-7-statement-of-cash-flows/)
is applied using direct receipts and payments and a separate exchange effect.

The software prepares a readable annual financial-statement illustration for
these facts. It does not file documents, give a legal compliance opinion, issue
an auditor's report, waive an audit, or substitute for directors' approval.
Whether a real corporation needs an audit or filing depends on its actual facts.
The synthetic authorisation metadata is a scenario input, not a real approval.

## Run and inspect

```sh
ifrs-close --statutory --input examples/statutory_group --output output/annual-close
```

`annual_financial_statements.md` is the assembled statement pack. It contains
comparative classified financial position, income/OCI, direct cash flows, changes
in each equity component for both years, the numerical tax reconciliation, and
notes. The same tables are exported separately as `annual_*.csv`; `annual_notes.md`
contains the narrative and comparative supporting disclosures.

My checked-in [example report](../examples/statutory_group/report/annual_financial_statements.md)
is generated by this command. The authoritative data are the input files and
reconciliation checks, not the rounded Markdown display.

## Sources and blocking checks

- `opening_config.json` / `opening_ledger.csv`: 31 October 2024 balances.
- `comparative_config.json` / `comparative_ledger.csv`: 2025 annual results and closing balances.
- `config.json` / `ledger.csv`: 2026 annual results and closing balances.
- `cash_movements.csv`: signed local cash receipts/payments at transaction-date rates,
  by year/entity/category, including reciprocal references for internal transfers.
- `equity_movements.csv`: explicit contributions and dividends by year/entity.
  Both are zero in this profile; a nonzero amount is rejected rather than dropped.
- `statutory.json`: entity identity, reporting dates, jurisdiction, accounting basis,
  material policies, share rights/count, tax rates and loss usage, control,
  authorisation, risks, going concern, contingencies and subsequent events.

All three ledgers are independently consolidated by the original engine. The
annual layer requires consecutive year-ends, an unchanged group/currency scope,
opening retained earnings equal to the comparative closing amount, original
capital carried forward, and opening CTA equal to the previous closing reserve.
Each entity's local cash change must equal its movement schedule. Internal cash
transfers need reciprocal matching references and equal translated values.

The group cash statement then removes internal transfers. Gross external receipts
must match recognised external revenue; supplier payments must match costs; tax
paid must match tax expense for this fully settled service-business profile.
Operating cash is independently checked to profit. Each entity's exchange effect
is the difference between its rate-translated opening/closing cash and validated
transaction-rate movements. It is not an operating cash plug. The cash bridge
and every equity component reconcile to the financial position.

Missing disclosures or schedules, unsupported material accounts, source mismatches,
and tax reconciliations that do not equal ledger expenses block **all** exports,
including the base statements. Ten input file hashes are stored for reproducibility.

## Presentation and relevant disclosures

The position separates current and non-current assets and liabilities. Cash is
current, original investments are eliminated, and there are no external liabilities
or non-current balances in this service-only profile. A broader business with
maturing debt needs an explicit maturity allocation rather than this profile.

Notes cover consolidation and currency policies; completed-service revenue
recognition; cash measurement and credit risk; currency/liquidity exposure; capital
management; share rights; control and subsidiaries; related parties and zero key
management compensation; tax-rate reconciliation and comparative loss relief;
no deferred tax; no contingencies, restrictions or commitments; going concern;
subsequent events; and issued standards not yet applied. Quantitative note amounts
and country revenue are drawn from the consolidated ledgers. Narrative disclosures
are mandatory preparer inputs, not inferred from a trial balance. Their truth still
requires human review.

The profile assumes unrestricted demand deposits, no external receivables or
payables, completed services, no investing/financing/non-cash transactions, no
leases, pensions, goodwill, NCI or distributions, and no tax timing/permanent
differences. All income tax is paid in-year; prior available losses are fully used.
Tax rates are scenario assumptions, not an automated tax-return calculation.
Unsupported material facts require extending the model and its disclosures first.

## Independent numerical expectations

Opening 2024 group cash/equity is CAD 50,000. For 2025, CAD 17,200 receipts less
CAD 12,800 payments gives CAD 4,400 profit and operating cash. Rates equal the
historical equity rates, so CTA and cash exchange effects are zero. Cash and equity
close at CAD 54,400, including CAD 4,400 retained earnings. Prior-year loss relief
is CAD 462 US + 420 Germany + 200 UK = 1,082 of tax; tax expense is zero.

In 2026 external revenue is 1,200 + 15,000 + 13,500 + 12,250 = CAD 41,950.
Costs are 500 + 10,000 + 9,000 + 7,350 = CAD 26,850; pretax profit is CAD 15,100.
The entity tax expenses are CAD 185.50 + 1,060.50 + 810 + 1,662.50 = CAD 3,718.50,
leaving CAD 11,381.50 profit and operating cash. A USD 240 receipt and EUR 200
payment both translate to CAD 300 and are eliminated from cash flows.

Opening foreign cash translated at new rates creates exchange effects of
CAD 1,800 US + 900 Germany + 975 UK = 3,675. Closing group cash is therefore
54,400 + 11,381.50 + 3,675 = CAD 69,456.50. Retained earnings become
4,400 + 11,381.50 = CAD 15,781.50; share capital remains CAD 50,000 and CTA
becomes CAD 3,675. Their sum is CAD 69,456.50. The financial position, cash and
equity bridges agree independently.

For this annual fixture rates change at the start of 2026 and are stable within
each year; transaction, average and closing rates therefore coincide within a year.
The original monthly fixture and its tests continue to exercise different average
and closing rates. Annual planning CSVs are forecasts, not statutory statements.
